STATISTICAL ASSESSMENT & HYPOTHESIS TESTING

A trend line is only the beginning.

A visible change does not automatically establish a statistically significant trend. EARTHSCOPE combines trend estimation and statistical testing to help users distinguish observed patterns from statistical evidence. Evaluates serial persistence via Lag-1 autocorrelation (r₁), applies Yue-Pilon pre-whitening if serial dependence is active, and controls family-wise false discoveries via Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q-values).

The Science Behind Every Result

Understand the data, statistical methods, assumptions, and limitations behind EARTHSCOPE investigations. EARTHSCOPE is designed to distinguish observed environmental patterns from statistically evaluated trends. Each result retains its dataset provenance, processing decisions, analytical methodology, and limitations.

Earth Observation Data

NASA satellite missions (MODIS Terra/Aqua, GPM constellation, Aura) combined with atmospheric reanalysis models (MERRA-2, GLDAS). Sensor measurements are rigorously separated from reanalysis products.

Variable Definitions

Documented physical indicators including canopy indices (NDVI, EVI), skin temperature (Day/Night LST in °C), calibrated precipitation (GPM IMERG V07B), and 2-meter air temperature ($T_{2\text{m}}$).

Spatial Selection & Geometry

Geospatial bounding coordinates validated against WGS 84 (EPSG:4326). Regional aggregation computes spatial averages across valid, quality-flagged grid cells.

Quality Control (QA Bitmasks)

Cloud, cloud shadow, cirrus, and high-aerosol contamination flagged and removed via 16-bit DetailedQA (MOD13Q1) and QC_Day/Night (MOD11A2) flags.

Trend Estimation (Sen's Slope)

Non-parametric median slope $\beta = \text{median}\{(x_j - x_k)/(j - k)\}$, resilient to anomalous seasonal outliers, extreme floods, and missing intervals.

Mann-Kendall Significance

Evaluates monotonic upward or downward trends using rank correlation $S$ and normal $Z$-score with exact tie corrections at $\alpha = 0.05$.

Autocorrelation & Yue-Pilon

Evaluates lag-1 serial persistence ($r_1$). If $|r_1| > 0.1$, monotonic slope is removed, residual AR(1) filtered, and slope blended back before running tests.

Multiple Hypothesis Testing (FDR)

Controls False Discovery Rate across multiple simultaneous environmental variables using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure, reporting adjusted $q$-values.

Confidence & Uncertainty

Provides two-sided 95% rank-based Gilbert confidence bounds $[Q_{(M_1)}, Q_{(M_2)}]$ around Sen's median trajectory.

🛡️ Scientific Transparency & Independent Research Statement

EARTHSCOPE is an independent Earth-system trend discovery and analysis platform. It uses data products made available by scientific data providers and applies documented geospatial and statistical methods to investigate environmental changes.

Results depend on dataset characteristics, observation coverage, quality filtering, spatial aggregation, temporal resolution, and statistical assumptions.

Scientific Caveat: Statistical association does not establish causation. A statistically non-significant result does not demonstrate the absence of environmental change.

EXAMPLE INQUIRIES:
EXECUTIVE SCIENTIFIC FINDING
Analysis for Kodagu Forest Region (12.3375° N, 75.8069° E) confirms vegetation indices (NDVI) maintain long-term stability (-0.012/decade) with seasonal dip during pre-monsoonal thermal maxima.
OBSERVED PHYSICAL CHANGES
Near-surface temperatures (T2M) have increased by +0.28°C over the past 10 years (NASA POWER), while GPM IMERG precipitation totals show increased inter-annual variance with delayed monsoon onset.
STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Mann-Kendall monotonic trend test indicates p = 0.042 (significant at α=0.05). Autocorrelation lag-1 check (r₁ = 0.14) confirmed no significant serial dependence confounding.
POTENTIAL DRIVERS & ANOMALIES
Canopy water stress correlates strongly with soil moisture deficit in the 0-10cm root zone (SMAP L4, r = 0.68), indicating meteorological drying rather than active commercial deforestation.
SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS
Optical satellite clear-sky scenes during July-August are sparse due to monsoon cloud cover (>70% cloud fraction). Missing scenes were masked via Scene Classification Layer rather than interpolated.
PROVIDER CITATIONS
NASA POWER (v2.0.0), Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A (ESA), NASA GPM IMERG Final Run, NASA SMAP L4 Global Surface Soil Moisture.
